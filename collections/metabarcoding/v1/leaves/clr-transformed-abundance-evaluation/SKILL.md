# clr-transformed-abundance-evaluation

## Summary

Evaluates how well the significant ASVs called by each differential abundance tool (e.g., ANCOM-II, ALDEx2, LEfSe, edgeR) on the 38 filtered 16S rRNA datasets discriminate between the two sample groups, by treating tool calls as prediction scores and computing AUROC, precision, recall, and F1 separately on relative abundance and CLR-transformed inputs.

## When to use

Use when you have significant-ASV result tables from differential abundance tools run on two-group datasets and want to quantify each tool's discriminative power (AUROC, precision, recall, F1) on relative abundance vs CLR-transformed data.

## When NOT to use

- The goal is cross-study consistency of significant genera (e.g., obesity or diarrhea dataset analyses) rather than within-dataset discrimination between sample groups
- The goal is estimating the practical false-positive rate on real data; the article notes its randomized FDR approach is not a perfect representation of real data and that few significant ASVs is not proof of low practical FDR
- You need a single-tool recommendation; the authors state they cannot make a direct simple recommendation of a specific tool based on this analysis

## Inputs

- Significant-ASV result tables from each DA tool on the 38 filtered datasets (output of run_0.1_filt_datasets.sh)
- False-positive analysis tables for filtered datasets (output of run_all_FP_datasets_filt.sh)
- ASV abundance tables in relative-abundance form and CLR-transformed form

## Outputs

- Per-tool mean AUROC for relative abundance and CLR input types
- Summary TSV of precision, recall, and F1 per tool at AUC thresholds 0.7 and 0.9
- ROC curves and per-tool metric summary plots in the Supplementary Figs. 3-4 layout (ggplot2)

## How to apply

Load the significant-ASV result tables produced by the DA tools on the 38 filtered datasets (generated by run_0.1_filt_datasets.sh) together with the false-positive analysis tables (from run_all_FP_datasets_filt.sh) in R (version 3.6.3 with dplyr 0.8.5 and ggplot2 3.3.0). For each tool, treat its significant-ASV calls as prediction scores and compute per-ASV ROC curves and AUC values using ROCR, running the computation separately on relative-abundance inputs and on CLR-transformed abundance inputs. Aggregate the per-ASV AUCs to obtain each tool's mean AUROC for each input type. Binarize performance at AUC thresholds of 0.7 and 0.9 and compute precision, recall, and F1 per tool, expecting e.g. ANCOM-II and ALDEx2 to reach the highest precision at the 0.7 threshold on relative abundance (medians 0.99 and 0.82). Plot the ROC curves and per-tool metric summaries with ggplot2 to reproduce the Supplementary Fig. 3-4 layout.

## Related tools

- **ROCR** (Computes per-ASV ROC curves and AUC values from tool significant-ASV calls treated as prediction scores)
- **dplyr** (Data wrangling and aggregation of per-ASV AUCs into per-tool mean AUROC and metric summaries)

- **ANCOM-II** (One of the DA tools whose significant-ASV calls are scored; attains the highest precision (median 0.99 at AUROC threshold 0.7 on relative abundance, 1.0 on CLR)) — https://github.com/nearinj/Comparison_of_DA_microbiome_methods

## Examples

```
library(ROCR); pred <- prediction(asv_pred_scores, asv_labels); auc <- performance(pred, "auc")@y.values[[1]]
```

## Evaluation signals

- The summary TSV exists and contains an entry for every DA tool under both input types (relative abundance and CLR)
- Computed mean AUROC, precision, recall, and F1 match the reported Supplementary Fig. 3-4 values: ANCOM-II precision 0.99 and ALDEx2 precision 0.82 at threshold 0.7 on relative abundance
- At AUROC threshold 0.7 on CLR data, ANCOM-II and ALDEx2 medians reach 1.0 and 0.83 precision respectively
- Wilcoxon on CLR-transformed data reaches an F1 of 0.70
- Tools with high precision (ANCOM-II, ALDEx2) show lower recall than LEfSe and edgeR, matching the reported trade-off

## Limitations

- The randomized approach for estimating FDR is not a perfect representation of real data; real sample groupings likely contain systematic abundance differences, so few significant ASVs under this approach is not proof of low practical FDR
- The 10% prevalence filtering threshold (-F 0.1) was arbitrarily selected; the optimal cut-off is unknown
- Results depend on data pre-processing; this evaluation is specific to the 10%-prevalence-filtered 38-dataset pipeline and both relative abundance and CLR input types must be computed separately
